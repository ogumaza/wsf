WSF 1.4.0 plays the reverb and other effects of six games. It rips each arrangement of a sequence, for games that play one sequence in several ways. It also finds sound archives inside CRI CPK archives. A sound archive taken out of a WiiWare game now rips too. Game mode now plays Link's Crossbow Training, New Play Control! Donkey Kong Jungle Beat, New Play Control! Pikmin, New Play Control! Pikmin 2, Super Mario Galaxy and Super Mario Galaxy 2.

WSF rips Nintendo Wii sequenced music into a PSF-family format and plays it with the game's DSP program on an emulated GameCube/Wii DSP. The release comes with a ripper (`wsfrip`), a renderer (`wsfplay`), a foobar2000 component and an XMPlay plugin. `docs/wsf.md` describes the format.

The programs run on Windows 10 or later and require no runtime DLLs. To install the component, open the file with foobar2000, or add it under Preferences › Components. To install the XMPlay plugin, unzip `xmp-wsf-1.4.0.zip` and put `xmp-wsf.dll` in XMPlay's folder, or in a folder inside it. Then restart XMPlay. For macOS, the README explains how to build from source.

## New in 1.4.0

- **The games' reverb and other effects.** Games put reverbs, choruses and delays on the Wii's three auxiliary buses. WSF now plays them for Wii Sports, Wii Play, WarioWare: Smooth Moves, Animal Crossing: City Folk, Mario Kart Wii and Wii Music (USA), in both modes. Both modes play WSF's models of the effects. Game mode sets them up with the game's code. The models give the same samples as the game's code and run much faster. Smooth Moves' music picks its reverb as it plays, just as in the game. Mario Kart Wii and Wii Music change the reverb from scene to scene. Each rip gets the reverb of the scene that plays its sound. A rip that ends now plays until its effects fall silent. Rips of other games still play without effects. `wsfrip` says so. A player older than 1.4.0 plays an archive-mode rip without its effects. It plays a game-mode rip with them: the rip holds the code that sets them up.
- **Arrangements.** Some games play one sequence in several arrangements. They play only some of its tracks, change its tempo or set variables that change what it plays. A town's music, for example, can change from district to district. In archive mode, `wsfrip --tracks`, `--tempo` and `--var` rip a sequence in such an arrangement. The rip's tags `wsf_tracks`, `wsf_tempo` and `wsf_var` record it. The length analysis plays it too. `--suffix` adds a text to the rips' titles and file names. Each arrangement of a sound then rips as a separate file. The rips share one library. `--var` also rips sequences that play nothing until the game sets one of their variables. The foobar2000 component keeps the three tags when it edits or removes a file's tags. Players older than 1.4.0 ignore the tags and play the whole sequence.
- **Sound archives in CPK archives.** Some games keep their sound archive inside an archive of CRI Middleware's file system, a CPK (`.cpk`). `wsfrip` now finds and rips the sound archives inside a game's CPK archives. It decompresses those that CRILAYLA compresses. A CPK that lists its files by ID only, without their names, isn't read.
- **A WiiWare game's code for a loose sound archive.** A sound archive requires its game's code, for AX's microcode and the build of the sound library. A WiiWare game has no file named `main.dol`. A sound archive taken out of one therefore couldn't be ripped. `--microcode` now takes a game's unpacked title or extracted disc, or the main program as a title stores it. The main program may be compressed with the CX library.
- **Code page 932 on discs.** `wsfrip` decodes a disc's Shift-JIS text with code page 932, Windows' version of Shift-JIS. Circled digits, Roman numerals and the other characters that Windows adds now decode. They used to come out as `%XX`. Seven symbols, such as the wave dash, now decode as they do on Windows.
- **Link's Crossbow Training.** Game mode now plays Link's Crossbow Training (USA, revision 1). The game's sound library is JAudio2, as in Twilight Princess. The rips have the game's echo. `wsfrip --bgm` rips 148 of the game's 177 pieces of music. The other 29 make no sound when they play alone. All 29 are left over from Twilight Princess. The game's files hold no samples for 21 of them.
- **New Play Control! Donkey Kong Jungle Beat.** Game mode now plays New Play Control! Donkey Kong Jungle Beat (USA). It's the third game in WSF whose sound library is JAudio2. Its music and its sound effects rip. Music starts in the game's BGM player. The player sets each track of a piece as the game does when the piece starts. The game's sounds for the Wii Remote's speaker aren't ripped. The rips require a player of 1.4.0 or later. Earlier players can't start them.
- **New Play Control! Pikmin.** Game mode now plays New Play Control! Pikmin (USA). The game's sound library is the early JAudio1 of the GameCube release. `wsfrip` rips the game's sequenced music: 13 pieces, and the 8 parts of the piece that the cutscenes play. Each part rips on its own. The game's streamed music isn't ripped. Like Twilight Princess's rips, these require a player newer than WSF 1.1.0.
- **New Play Control! Pikmin 2.** Game mode now plays New Play Control! Pikmin 2 (USA). The game's sound library is the later JAudio1 of the GameCube release. `wsfrip` rips the game's 48 pieces of music and its sound effects. The effects include the Home Button menu's sounds. A piece plays as the game plays it with nothing going on: the layers of the music for work, treasure, enemies and battle stay quiet. A cave's piece plays with the first conductor of its floors. The conductor picks the phrases at random. The piece never repeats, and its rip plays for 10 minutes. The game's streamed music isn't ripped. Like Twilight Princess's rips, these require a player newer than WSF 1.1.0.
- **Super Mario Galaxy.** Game mode now plays Super Mario Galaxy (USA). Like Twilight Princess, the game uses the JAudio2 sound library. The game streams most of its music. `wsfrip --bgm` rips the 45 pieces that the game plays from sequences, such as its jingles and the music of its power-ups. The other 60 `BGM_*` sounds go with streamed music and make no sound alone. 62 pieces play a stream together with a sequence, and `wsfrip` can't rip them. The rips have no echo. The game adds echo only in some places.
- **Super Mario Galaxy 2.** Game mode now plays Super Mario Galaxy 2 (USA). Like Super Mario Galaxy, the game streams most of its music. `wsfrip --bgm` rips the 50 pieces that the game plays from sequences, such as its jingles, the music of its power-ups and Yoshi's dash. Of the other 77 `BGM_*` sounds, 76 go with streamed music and make no sound alone. The game's sound table gives the last one a volume of 0. 76 pieces play a stream together with a sequence, and `wsfrip` can't rip them. `--bgm` also takes the sound effects `SE_SY_LV_TITLE_BGM` and `SE_SY_LV_TITLE_BGM_RHYTHM` for their names. They hold a looping wave until the game stops them. Their rips play for 10 minutes.

## Features

- **Ripping.** `wsfrip` rips a Wii disc image (ISO, NKit, RVZ, WIA, GCZ, WBFS, CISO or WDF), an extracted disc, an unpacked WiiWare title, or a sound archive (`.brsar`, with its game's code). It creates a `.wsflib` and a `.miniwsf` for each sequence. `wsfrip` also finds the sound archives inside a game's CPK archives. The game partition must be stored decrypted. NKit, RVZ and WIA images store it that way. Dolphin converts other images to RVZ without losing data. `wsfrip` plays each sound to determine its length and fade tags. `--bgm` rips only the music. In archive mode, `--tracks`, `--tempo` and `--var` rip a sequence in an arrangement that a game plays it in. Drag and drop also works.
- **Two rip modes.** Game mode runs the game's sound code on an emulated Wii. The rips play as the game does. Game mode requires a driver profile for the game. Wii Sports, Wii Play, WarioWare: Smooth Moves, Mario Kart Wii, Wii Music and Animal Crossing: City Folk (all USA) have one. Another release whose code matches gets a profile derived from it. Five games whose sound library is JAudio2 have a profile too: The Legend of Zelda: Twilight Princess (USA, revision 2), Link's Crossbow Training (USA, revision 1), New Play Control! Donkey Kong Jungle Beat, Super Mario Galaxy and Super Mario Galaxy 2 (all USA). So do New Play Control! Pikmin and New Play Control! Pikmin 2 (both USA). Their sound library is JAudio1. Archive mode plays a sound archive from any game with WSF's model of the SDK's sound player. The model follows the 2006, 2007 and 2008 versions of the sound library.
- **The game's mixing.** In both modes, the game's DSP program runs on an emulated DSP: AX's microcode, or JAudio's DSP program. The mixing, filtering and resampling therefore match the console.
- **Effects.** WSF plays the reverb and other effects that Wii Sports, Wii Play, WarioWare: Smooth Moves, Animal Crossing: City Folk, Mario Kart Wii and Wii Music (all USA) put on the Wii's auxiliary buses, in both modes. WSF's models of the effects give the same samples as the game's code. The rips of Twilight Princess and Link's Crossbow Training have the games' echo.
- **Rendering.** `wsfplay` renders rips to WAV or FLAC at the Wii's rate of 32,000 Hz.
- **foobar2000.** The component plays `.miniwsf` files with their length and fade tags. It supports tag editing, ReplayGain and endless playback. The component renders ahead of playback on a separate thread and saves the emulation's state as it goes. Seeking back is instant. Seeking forward only waits for rendering to reach the target.
- **XMPlay.** The plugin plays `.miniwsf` files with their length and fade tags. It supports endless playback. Like the component, it renders ahead of playback and saves the emulation's state as it goes.

## Known limitations

- Archive mode's model follows the versions of the sound library in WarioWare: Smooth Moves (2006), Wii Sports (2007), Mario Kart Wii (March 2008) and Wii Music (June 2008). The model also follows two changes in versions from November 2008 and September 2009. `docs/wsf.md` lists them. A game with another version may play differently from the console. Versions from September 2009 on can also move a track's pitch bend over a set time. Archive mode sets the bend at once. The versions from June 2008 on can put biquad filters on a track's notes. Archive mode applies the library's preset filters. It doesn't apply a filter that a game defines in its code. Some games' AX microcode is a version that the model can't drive. Those games can only be ripped in game mode. Game mode requires a profile for the game.
- Game mode for another release of Mario Kart Wii, Wii Music or Animal Crossing: City Folk requires a profile made for it. Their version of the sound library checks a flag. The flag's address can't be found the way the functions are.
- Sounds that play until the game stops them, such as ambiences, get two loops and a fade when the ripper can find their loops. Otherwise they play until their notes end, for at most ten minutes.
- Only sequences are ripped. Use tools such as vgmstream for BRSTM streams. Wave sounds, and sequences whose data the game can't load, are skipped.
- The output is the TV's stereo mix. The Wii Remote speaker's sounds and surround aren't played.
- Effects play only for the six games under "Effects" above. Rips of other NintendoWare games have the dry mix. `wsfrip` says so.
- On a slow computer, rips may not play in real time. That can happen in both modes, in the foobar2000 component for 64-bit or 32-bit foobar2000, and in the XMPlay plugin. Archive mode is the lighter mode. The XMPlay plugin and the 32-bit component run the emulation more slowly than the 64-bit programs.
- Archive mode doesn't play JAudio. `wsfrip` therefore rips only the JAudio games and releases that have a profile. For any other JAudio2 game or release, `wsfrip` stops with an error that says so.
- Encrypted disc images and WADs aren't read. A WiiWare title's sound archives are read from the U8 archives among its contents, and from CPK archives in those. Other archives inside the U8 archives aren't read.
- A CPK archive that lists its files by ID only, without their names, isn't read.
