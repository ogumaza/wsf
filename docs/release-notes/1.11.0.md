WSF 1.11.0 rips New Play Control! Donkey Kong Jungle Beat's music in each state that the game sets during play. Many of the game's looping sound effects now end after two loops, and whole rips finish sooner.

WSF rips Nintendo Wii sequenced music into a PSF-family format and plays it with the game's DSP program on an emulated GameCube/Wii DSP. The release comes with a ripper (`wsfrip`), a renderer (`wsfplay`), a foobar2000 component and an XMPlay plugin. `docs/wsf.md` describes the format.

The programs run on Windows 10 or later and require no runtime DLLs. To install the component, open the file with foobar2000, or add it under Preferences › Components. To install the XMPlay plugin, unzip `xmp-wsf-1.11.0.zip` and put `xmp-wsf.dll` in XMPlay's folder, or in a folder inside it. Then restart XMPlay. For macOS, the README explains how to build from source.

## New in 1.11.0

- **New Play Control! Donkey Kong Jungle Beat's music in the game's states.** The game adds layers to its music as you play: a combo, a flight with a Helibird and each part of a boss fight turn more tracks on or change them. A rip of Jungle Beat now contains 102 variants of 44 pieces, one for each later state, beside each piece as the game starts it. A variant is named after the piece and what sets its state, for example `JA_BGM_MAIN_01_combo1` and `_combo2` for the layers of a combo, `_helibird` for a flight with a Helibird, `_round2` and `_change` for a boss's second round and the change before it, and `_underwater` for TropicalCoast's music while DK swims below. The states are those of the GameCube release, plus `JA_BGM_COURSE_SELECT_state1`, the map's music with two tracks off. Each variant plays its state from the start of the piece. `docs/wsf.md` lists the states and the stages that play each piece.

## Fixed in 1.11.0

- **Jungle Beat's looping effects.** Many of the game's sound effects loop in tracks that the effect's first track waits for. The ripper didn't find those loops, and their rips played for ten minutes. They now get two loops and a fade, as other looping sounds do.
- **Seeking back.** After a seek back, the foobar2000 component and the XMPlay plugin could keep two saved states in one stretch of a piece. They then reached their limit of saved states sooner, and a later seek back could take up to twice as long. Each stretch now keeps one saved state.
- **Extracted discs with a folder that can't be opened.** `wsfrip` stopped listing an extracted disc's `files` folder at the first subfolder that it wasn't allowed to open. It gave no message and left out the files after that folder. It now goes on past such a folder.
- **The `wsf_sound` tag of a game-mode variant.** A variant's rip in game mode, such as `SEQ_O_SELECT_state_1` from Mario Kart Wii or `Z2BGM_FIELD_LINK_DAY_riding` from Twilight Princess, gave the variant's name in `wsf_sound`. The tag now gives the sound's label, as in archive mode.

## Other changes

- **Faster rips.** The ripper now starts the length analyses that take longest first. They no longer run on alone at the end of a rip. The emulated CPU and DSP also run faster during the analysis. The README's new section, "Ripping faster", explains `--bgm`, `--only`, `--archive`, `--jobs` and `--no-length`.

## Features

- **Ripping.** `wsfrip` rips a Wii disc image (ISO, NKit, RVZ, WIA, GCZ, WBFS, CISO or WDF), an extracted disc, an unpacked title (a WiiWare game or a channel), or a sound archive (`.brsar`, with its game's code). It creates a `.wsflib` and a `.miniwsf` for each sequence, or for each piece of music that a game plays as several sequences together. `wsfrip` also finds the sound archives inside a game's CPK archives. The game partition must be stored decrypted. NKit, RVZ and WIA images store it that way. Dolphin converts other images to RVZ without losing data. `wsfrip` plays each sound to determine its length and fade tags. `--bgm` rips only the music. In archive mode, `--tracks`, `--tempo` and `--var` rip a sequence in an arrangement that a game plays it in. Drag and drop also works.
- **Two rip modes.** Game mode runs the game's sound code on an emulated Wii. The rips play as the game does. Game mode requires a driver profile for the game. Wii Sports, Wii Play, WarioWare: Smooth Moves, Mario Kart Wii, Wii Music and Animal Crossing: City Folk (all USA) have one. Another release whose code matches gets a profile derived from it. Five games whose sound library is JAudio2 have a profile too: The Legend of Zelda: Twilight Princess (USA revisions 0 and 2, Europe and Japan), Link's Crossbow Training (USA revision 1, Europe and Japan), New Play Control! Donkey Kong Jungle Beat (USA), and Super Mario Galaxy and Super Mario Galaxy 2 (USA, Europe and Japan). So do New Play Control! Pikmin and New Play Control! Pikmin 2 (both USA). Their sound library is JAudio1. Archive mode plays a sound archive from any game with WSF's model of the SDK's sound player. The model follows the 2006, 2007 and 2008 versions of the sound library.
- **The game's mixing.** In both modes, the game's DSP program runs on an emulated DSP: AX's microcode, or JAudio's DSP program. The mixing, filtering and resampling therefore match the console.
- **Effects.** WSF plays the reverb and other effects that Wii Sports, Wii Play, WarioWare: Smooth Moves, Animal Crossing: City Folk, Mario Kart Wii and Wii Music (all USA) put on the Wii's auxiliary buses, in both modes. The European releases of these six games and the Japanese release of WarioWare: Smooth Moves get the same effects. Rips of five Wii channels have the channels' effects: the reverb of the Wii Shop, Mii and Photo 1.1 channels (all World) and of the Forecast and News channels (USA), and the Photo Channel's mood effect. Rips of Pokémon Rumble (WiiWare, USA) have its reverb, delay and chorus in archive mode. WSF's models of the effects give the same samples as the game's code. The rips of Twilight Princess and Link's Crossbow Training have the games' echo.
- **Rendering.** `wsfplay` renders rips to WAV or FLAC at the Wii's rate of 32,000 Hz.
- **foobar2000.** The component plays `.miniwsf` files with their length and fade tags. It supports tag editing, ReplayGain and endless playback. The component renders ahead of playback on a separate thread and saves the emulation's state as it goes. Seeking back is instant. Seeking forward only waits for rendering to reach the target.
- **XMPlay.** The plugin plays `.miniwsf` files with their length and fade tags. It supports endless playback. Like the component, it renders ahead of playback and saves the emulation's state as it goes.

## Known limitations

- Archive mode's model follows the versions of the sound library in WarioWare: Smooth Moves (2006), Wii Sports (2007), Mario Kart Wii (March 2008) and Wii Music (June 2008). The model also follows the changes of the versions from November 2008 and September 2009. `docs/wsf.md` lists them. A game with another version may play differently from the console. The versions from June 2008 on can put biquad filters on a track's notes. Archive mode applies the library's preset filters. It doesn't apply a filter that a game defines in its code. Some games' AX microcode is a version that the model can't drive. Those games can only be ripped in game mode. Game mode requires a profile for the game.
- A rip of a state plays that state from the music's start. The game's fades from one state to another have no rip. Some states play a track at a lower volume than the rip, such as the carousel of Wii Music's main menu. `docs/wsf.md` lists them.
- A rip of one of Jungle Beat's chord effects plays it over the music of the first stage in the game's table of stages. The effect starts with the keys that the game holds after its start-up. In the game, the keys follow the stage, the chord of the moment and the keys that came before.
- Sounds that play until the game stops them, such as ambiences, get two loops and a fade when the ripper can find their loops. Otherwise they play until their notes end, for at most ten minutes.
- Mega Man 9 silences a channel's music while a sound effect holds that channel. The rips play no sound effects, so the music plays on.
- Only sequences are ripped. Use tools such as vgmstream for BRSTM streams. Wave sounds, and sequences whose data the game can't load, are skipped.
- The output is the TV's stereo mix. The Wii Remote speaker's sounds and surround aren't played.
- Effects play only for the games, releases and channels under "Effects" above. Rips of other NintendoWare games, and of the other releases of those games, have the dry mix. `wsfrip` says so.
- On a slow computer, rips may not play in real time. That can happen in both modes, in the foobar2000 component for 64-bit or 32-bit foobar2000, and in the XMPlay plugin. Archive mode is the lighter mode. The XMPlay plugin and the 32-bit component run the emulation more slowly than the 64-bit programs.
- Archive mode doesn't play JAudio. `wsfrip` therefore rips only the JAudio games and releases that have a profile. For any other JAudio2 game or release, `wsfrip` stops with an error that says so.
- Encrypted disc images and WADs aren't read. A title's sound archives are read from the U8 archives among its contents, and from CPK archives in those. Other archives inside the U8 archives aren't read.
- A CPK archive that lists its files by ID only, without their names, isn't read.
- A library holds a program of at most 1 GiB. A game-mode library of a game that uses NW4R SND keeps the size of the game's sound archive. Such a game with an archive over 1 GiB rips only in archive mode.
