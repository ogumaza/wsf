WSF 1.13.0 rips the music of Super Mario Galaxy and Super Mario Galaxy 2 in the states that the games put it in as you play, and eight more of the enemy loops of New Play Control! Donkey Kong Jungle Beat. The rips and the players now follow soundtrack albums: a loop gets loop tags and a 7-second fade, and a track plays from its first sound, with a short silence before and after it.

WSF rips Nintendo Wii sequenced music into a PSF-family format and plays it with the game's DSP program on an emulated GameCube/Wii DSP. The release comes with a ripper (`wsfrip`), a renderer (`wsfplay`), a foobar2000 component and an XMPlay plugin. `docs/wsf.md` describes the format.

For macOS, the README explains how to build from source.

## New in 1.13.0

- **The states of the Galaxy games' music.** As you play, Super Mario Galaxy and Super Mario Galaxy 2 put some pieces in other states: the Star Ball's speeds, riding Yoshi, a Dash Pepper or a Blimp Fruit, Bowser's escape, a galaxy picked on the map of the dome, and others. A state turns tracks on or off, and some states also change the tempo, the pitch of the tracks or the volume of one track. A rip now contains each state that plays other notes as one more variant, named after the piece and the state, for example `BGM_TAMAKORO_speed_very_high`: 20 states of Galaxy's pieces and 35 of Galaxy 2's. Some pieces play a sequence over one of the streams. A state of such a piece rips the sequence alone, without its stream, and its name contains `_layer_`, for example `BGM_ASTRO_DOME_layer_rotate`. Each variant plays its state from the start of the piece. In the game, the piece moves into the state over up to three seconds. Rip the games again to get these variants.
- **More of Jungle Beat's enemy loops.** A rip of Jungle Beat now contains 135 variants of 27 pieces for the loops that enemies and objects play beside the music, where 1.12.0 ripped 127 of 25. In Survival Mode, the final stage plays `JA_BGM_BOSS_FINAL` in place of the stage music, and `JA_BGM_BOSS_FINAL_enemy_angry`, `JA_BGM_BOSS_FINAL_dk_wait_attack` and `JA_BGM_BOSS_FINAL_attack_chance` play its loops over that piece. The main game's final fight also switches to `JA_BGM_BOSS_FINAL` once it starts, and `JA_BGM_BOSS_FINAL_dk_wait_attack` is the music of that part of the fight too. A pig that faints plays the attack chance over the music of four stages: `JA_BGM_MAIN_01_attack_chance`, `JA_BGM_MAIN_04_attack_chance`, `JA_BGM_MAIN_21_attack_chance` and `JA_BGM_MAIN_22_attack_chance`. `JA_BGM_BOSS_GOROPY_B_attack_chance` plays the attack chance of a coconut that DK hits in a GoroPig fight. `docs/wsf.md` lists the loops and the objects that play them. Rip Jungle Beat again to get these variants.

## Fixed in 1.13.0

- **Jungle Beat's chord effects a frame late.** The driver of the ripper ran the audio frame of the game before the rhythm. The rips of chord effects, and of the layers that play them, therefore started each step of a chord a frame later than the game does. The driver now runs the two in the order of the game.
- **The ceremony's lecture layer.** `JA_BGM_OP_CEMEMONY_lect_kikki_run` ripped without its music, because the first state of `JA_BGM_OP_CEMEMONY` turns every track off. The rip now plays the tracks of the piece as the rip of the piece alone does.

## Other changes

- **Lengths and fades as on a soundtrack album.** A loop now gets two passes and a 7-second fade, in place of a 10-second fade, and the new tags `loop_start` and `loop_length`: the time to the start of the loop and the length of one pass. A sound that ends gets its length at its last sound and a 1-second fade, in place of half a second of silence. The length counts from the first sound. Music that hasn't repeated after 10 minutes now plays on for up to an hour, and a loop longer than 10 minutes still gets two passes and the fade. Rip a game again to get the new tags.
- **The players play a track as a soundtrack album does.** `wsfplay`, the foobar2000 component and the XMPlay plugin skip the silence at the start of a track, play 0.5 s of silence before it and 1 s after it, and fade with the square of the time left in the fade, as most soundtrack albums do. Their settings change the silences, the number of loops and the fade of a loop and its curve. They can also play one pass of a long loop, ignore the length and fade tags, and skip the silence at the end. `wsfplay --help` lists its options, and `--raw` plays a file as it is.
- **The game tag.** `wsfrip` no longer takes the game's name from a table in its code. The `game` tag is the value of `--game`, or else the title in the disc header, or the title in the banner for a WiiWare game or a channel. For a game with a driver profile, the tag and the start of the file name of each library were the name of the profile, such as `Mario Kart Wii (USA)`. They're now the title in the disc header, such as `MarioKartWii`.
- **Other consoles' sound archives.** `wsfrip` now names the console and the format of a sound archive from another console, for example "error: X is a Switch sound archive (little-endian FSAR); WSF rips Wii sound archives (RSAR, .brsar)". It tells a Wii U archive from a Switch one. A DS sound archive (SDAT) now gets this error too. Before, `wsfrip` tried to read it as a disc image.

## Features

- **Ripping.** `wsfrip` rips a Wii disc image (ISO, NKit, RVZ, WIA, GCZ, WBFS, CISO or WDF), an extracted disc, an unpacked title (a WiiWare game or a channel), or a sound archive (`.brsar`, with its game's code). It creates a `.wsflib` and a `.miniwsf` for each sequence, or for each piece of music that a game plays as several sequences together. `wsfrip` also finds the sound archives inside a game's CPK archives. The game partition must be stored decrypted. NKit, RVZ and WIA images store it that way. Dolphin converts other images to RVZ without losing data. `wsfrip` plays each sound to determine its length, fade and loop tags. `--bgm` rips only the music. In archive mode, `--tracks`, `--tempo` and `--var` rip a sequence in an arrangement that a game plays it in. Drag and drop also works.
- **Two rip modes.** Game mode runs the game's sound code on an emulated Wii. The rips play as the game does. Game mode requires a driver profile for the game. Wii Sports, Wii Play, WarioWare: Smooth Moves, Mario Kart Wii, Wii Music and Animal Crossing: City Folk (all USA) have one. Another release whose code matches gets a profile derived from it. Five games whose sound library is JAudio2 have a profile too: The Legend of Zelda: Twilight Princess (USA revisions 0 and 2, Europe and Japan), Link's Crossbow Training (USA revision 1, Europe and Japan), New Play Control! Donkey Kong Jungle Beat (USA), and Super Mario Galaxy and Super Mario Galaxy 2 (USA, Europe and Japan). So do New Play Control! Pikmin and New Play Control! Pikmin 2 (both USA). Their sound library is JAudio1. Archive mode plays a sound archive from any game with WSF's model of the sound player in the SDK. The model follows the 2006, 2007 and 2008 versions of the sound library.
- **The game's mixing.** In both modes, the game's DSP program runs on an emulated DSP: AX's microcode, or JAudio's DSP program. The mixing, filtering and resampling therefore match the console.
- **Effects.** WSF plays the reverb and other effects that Wii Sports, Wii Play, WarioWare: Smooth Moves, Animal Crossing: City Folk, Mario Kart Wii and Wii Music (all USA) put on the Wii's auxiliary buses, in both modes. The European releases of these six games and the Japanese release of WarioWare: Smooth Moves get the same effects. Rips of five Wii channels have the effects of the channels: the reverb of the Wii Shop, Mii and Photo 1.1 channels (all World) and of the Forecast and News channels (USA), and the Photo Channel's mood effect. Rips of Pokémon Rumble (WiiWare, USA) have its reverb, delay and chorus in archive mode. WSF's models of the effects produce the same samples as the game's code. The rips of Twilight Princess and Link's Crossbow Training have the games' echo.
- **Rendering.** `wsfplay` renders rips to WAV or FLAC at the Wii's rate of 32,000 Hz, as a soundtrack album plays them.
- **foobar2000.** The component plays `.miniwsf` files with their length, fade and loop tags, as a soundtrack album plays them. It supports tag editing, ReplayGain and endless playback. The component renders ahead of playback on a separate thread and saves the state of the emulation as it goes. Seeking back is instant. Seeking forward only waits for rendering to reach the target.
- **XMPlay.** The plugin plays `.miniwsf` files with their length, fade and loop tags, as a soundtrack album plays them. It supports endless playback. Like the component, it renders ahead of playback and saves the state of the emulation as it goes.

## Known limitations

- Archive mode's model follows the versions of the sound library in WarioWare: Smooth Moves (2006), Wii Sports (2007), Mario Kart Wii (March 2008) and Wii Music (June 2008). The model also follows the changes of the versions from November 2008 and September 2009. `docs/wsf.md` lists them. A game with another version may play differently from the console. The versions from June 2008 on can put biquad filters on a track's notes. Archive mode applies the library's preset filters. It doesn't apply a filter that a game defines in its code. The AX microcode of some games is a version that the model can't drive. Those games can only be ripped in game mode. Game mode requires a profile for the game.
- A rip of a state plays that state from the start of the music. The game's fades from one state to another have no rip. A state of a Galaxy piece that plays a sequence over a stream rips the sequence without the stream. Some states play a track at a lower volume than the rip, such as the carousel of Wii Music's main menu. `docs/wsf.md` lists them.
- A rip of one of Jungle Beat's chord effects plays it over the music of the first stage in the game's table of stages. The effect starts with the keys that the game has after its start-up. In the game, the keys follow the stage, the chord of the moment and the keys that came before.
- Sounds that play until the game stops them, such as ambiences, get two loops and a fade when the ripper can find their loops. Otherwise they play until their notes end, for at most ten minutes.
- Mega Man 9 silences the music of a channel while a sound effect uses that channel. The rips play no sound effects, and the music plays on.
- Only sequences are ripped. Use tools such as vgmstream for BRSTM streams. Wave sounds, and sequences whose data the game can't load, are skipped.
- The output is the TV's stereo mix. The Wii Remote speaker's sounds and surround aren't played.
- Effects play only for the games, releases and channels under "Effects" above. Rips of other NintendoWare games, and of the other releases of those games, have the dry mix. `wsfrip` says so.
- On a slow computer, rips may not play in real time. That can happen in both modes, in the foobar2000 component for 64-bit or 32-bit foobar2000, and in the XMPlay plugin. Archive mode is the lighter mode. The XMPlay plugin and the 32-bit component run the emulation more slowly than the 64-bit programs.
- Archive mode doesn't play JAudio. `wsfrip` therefore rips only the JAudio games and releases that have a profile. For any other JAudio2 game or release, `wsfrip` stops with an error that says so.
- Encrypted disc images and WADs aren't read. A title's sound archives are read from the U8 archives among its contents, and from CPK archives in those. Other archives inside the U8 archives aren't read.
- A CPK archive that lists its files by ID only, without their names, isn't read.
- A library contains a program of at most 1 GiB. A game-mode library of a game that uses NW4R SND keeps the size of the game's sound archive. Such a game with an archive over 1 GiB rips only in archive mode.
